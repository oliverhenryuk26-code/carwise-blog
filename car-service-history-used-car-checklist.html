How to Review a Car Service History Before Buying a Used Car

A practical checklist for used-car buyers

A used car can look excellent during a short inspection and still carry maintenance questions that deserve attention. A car service history does not replace a mechanical inspection, but it can help you understand how consistently the vehicle was cared for and where to ask better questions.

Start with the record itself

Ask to see workshop invoices, service-book stamps, digital records, parts receipts, or notes from previous owners. Useful records normally include dates, odometer readings, the work performed, and the workshop that carried it out.

Do not focus only on whether a folder is thick. A small number of clear, chronological invoices can be more useful than a large, disorganised stack of papers.

Check whether the mileage makes sense

Compare each recorded odometer reading with the next. The numbers should generally rise in a believable pattern over time. Long gaps, sudden changes, or records that do not line up with the dashboard reading are not automatic proof of a problem, but they are good reasons to ask for an explanation.

  • Look for dates that are in sequence.
  • Compare invoice mileage with the current odometer.
  • Ask about periods with no documented maintenance.
  • Consider whether low annual mileage fits the vehicle's condition and paperwork.

Look beyond routine oil changes

Regular servicing is encouraging, but major maintenance tells another part of the story. Depending on the model and its schedule, look for evidence of tyres, brakes, batteries, belts, fluids, suspension components, or other wear items.

Use gaps as prompts, not conclusions

Not every owner keeps every invoice, and cars may be serviced at home or at independent workshops. A missing record should lead to a practical question: what happened during that period, and is there another way to verify it? Pair the paperwork with a road test and an independent inspection where possible.

Where to continue your research

Before making a decision, review the available documentation alongside the vehicle itself. For additional context, explore Carwise's car service history information.

Final takeaway

A car service history is most useful when you read it as a timeline rather than a yes-or-no checklist. Look for sensible dates and mileage, understand what work was completed, and investigate gaps calmly.
